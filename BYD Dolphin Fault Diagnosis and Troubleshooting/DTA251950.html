Instructions and operations

Instructions

This section is provided to assist in the diagnosis, testing and maintenance related to noise, vibration and abnormal sound.

  1. Noise is defined as the sound that has nothing to do with the equipment in the cab but cannot be accepted by passengers.

  2. Vibration is defined as the fluctuation felt by passengers that is not caused by road surface changes.

  3. Comfort is a matter of riding quality as passengers can clearly feel the reaction from the road.

Principle of diagnosis

Diagnosis is not only based on a series of related steps to solve specific problems. It is a method to check a system that fails to function properly and find out the cause. It should also help to understand how the system works and whether it works normally. There are some basic rules for diagnosis. If these rules can be observed, the cause of a problem can usually be found out at the first time.

Understand the system

  1. Understand how the parts are assembled.

  2. Understand operations and restrictions of the system, and what will happen if the system goes wrong.

  3. Sometimes, it is necessary to check a possibly faulty system against a known good system.

Understand the history of the system

Any clue about the following items can save the diagnostic time:

  1. Is the system new or old?

  2. What kind of treatment has been done to the system?

  3. Has the system been repaired previously? Is the current situation related to the previous repair?

  4. How about the maintenance records?

Understand the history of the situation

Any clue about the following items can save the diagnostic time:

  1. Does the situation occur suddenly or gradually?

  2. Is the situation related to other events, such as impact or previous replacement of parts?

  3. Knowing how the situation was discovered may be an important clue to find out the cause.

Understand the development possibility of certain situations

Pay attention to simple problems instead of complex ones. For example:

  1. A circuit problem usually occurs in the connection, not in the part itself.

  2. Startup failure of the motor is more likely to be caused by loose wires or minor adjustments, rather than mechanical faults.

  3. It is necessary to understand the difference between "impossible" and the "unlikely". Faults in some systems, though unlikely, still happen.

  4. Although parts are new, it does not mean that they function properly.

Symptoms eliminated but failed to identify fault cause

For example, reducing the tire pressure of a front wheel may solve the vehicle's leaning to one side, but this does not eliminate the real cause.

Fault cause identified

Confirm the problem found repeatedly and check what causes the wear of components. For example:

  • Looseness of one fixed seat of the powertrain may indicate that other fixed seats also get loose.
