How to check connectors

Reminder

Many electrical faults may be caused by faults in electrical connections or wiring harnesses, or by the sticking of parts or relays. Check if the connector is well connected before confirming whether a fault is caused by parts or wiring harness assemblies.

  1. Check the connector with a multimeter

    1. Detection from the wiring harness side:

      • If the connector has a back cover, remove the back cover before detecting the terminal.

      • Do not detect a waterproof connector from the wiring harness side; otherwise, the seal between the wiring harness and the connector may be damaged.

    2. Detection from the terminal side:

      • Female terminal: Do not insert any object larger than the male terminal into the cathode connector.

      • Male terminal: Carefully detect the contact surface of each terminal with a T-probe, and do not bend the terminal.

  2. Check whether the tension of contact spring of the terminal is appropriate

    1. The contact spring at the terminal may generate intermittent signals in the circuit.

    2. If an intermittent open circuit occurs, check the contact spring at the open-circuited wiring harness and the female terminal in accordance with the following steps.

      • Use a male terminal that matches the female terminal.

      • Disconnect the suspected faulty connector and fix the terminal side upwards.

      • Try to insert the male terminal into the female terminal when fixing the male terminal wiring harness.

      • Plug and unplug the connector again, and check whether the male terminal can be easily inserted into the female terminal.
