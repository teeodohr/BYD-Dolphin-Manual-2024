Circuit simulation tests

  1. Occasional faults and other faults that cannot be detected by road test may be detected by circuit simulation tests, and possible vehicle faults can be effectively determined by simulating the conditions/environments when such faults occur.

    Reminder

    • Listening carefully to the customer's description of the fault is very important for simulating the conditions when the fault symptoms occur.

  2. Simulation tests are generally divided into the following 6 categories:

      • Vehicle vibration test

      • Thermal sensitivity test

      • Freeze test

      • Leak test

      • Load test

      • Voltage drop test

    • Vehicle vibration test: Faults may occur when the vehicle is running on a rough road. In this case, it is necessary to check the vibration-related conditions and the following parts of the vehicle:

      1. Connectors and wiring harness: Determine which connectors and wiring harness may affect the electrical system under inspection. Gently vibrate or swing each connector and wiring harness while observing whether a fault subject to a simulation attempt occurs in the system. This test may show loose or poor electrical connections.

        Reminder
        When a connector is exposed to a humid environment for a long time, a corrosive film may form on its terminals. This condition may not be found by visual inspection when the connector is not disconnected. If an intermittent fault occurs, it may be caused by corrosion. It is recommended to check and clean the terminals of the relevant connector in the system after disconnecting it.

      2. Sensors and relays: Gently vibrate the sensors and relays in the system under inspection. This test may identify loose or poorly-installed sensors or relays.

      3. There are many reasons for electrical faults arising from vehicle vibration, among which the following need to be checked:

        Reminder
        First, make sure that the system is properly grounded, then gently vibrate the wiring harness or parts as per the method introduced earlier and check whether the connection gets loose, and finally check the continuity of the wiring harness and conduct a voltage drop test according to the circuit diagram.

        • The connector is not installed in place.

        • The wiring harness is not long enough and is stretched due to vibration or shaking.

        • The wiring harness is attached to the bracket or movable parts.

        • The GND wire is loose, dirty or corroded.

        • The wiring harness is too close to high-temperature parts.

      4. Locations behind the instrument panel

        • Improper fixing of wiring harness may cause them to be twisted together during the installation of accessories. Vehicle vibration can cause excessive wear of wiring harness near brackets or mounting screws.

        • When the vehicle vibrates, loose or unfixed wiring harness will get stuck by seat parts (such as sliding guide rails). If any wiring harness passes under the installation position, it is necessary to check whether the wiring harness is damaged or stuck.

    • Thermal sensitivity test

      1. Some faults may occur after the vehicle is exposed to sunlight in summer or is running for a long time. The cause may be related to the high temperature suffered by some parts of the wiring harness/electrical system. In this case, the thermal sensitivity test method shall be used.

      2. A heating gun or similar tool shall be used to heat the faulty system parts and conduct the thermal sensitivity test.

        Caution

        • Do not heat parts above 60 °C (140 °F).

        • If a fault occurs during the heating test of any parts, the parts should be properly insulated and replaced if necessary.

    • Freeze test

      1. If a vehicle fault disappears after the vehicle warms up in winter, the fault cause may be related to icing of some parts of the wiring harness/electrical system. Check whether this situation exists using the following two methods:

        • Method I: Leave the vehicle outside all night. Ensure that the temperature is low enough to allow recurrence of the fault, and diagnose the parts that may be affected quickly and thoroughly in the morning.

        • Method II: Put the suspected parts in the refrigerating chamber long enough to freeze, reinstall the parts on the vehicle and check whether the fault recurs. If the fault recurs, repair or replace the parts.

    • Leak test

      1. Check the cooling system for leakage.

      2. Check the A/C system for leakage.

      3. If the fault only occurs in highly-humid or rainy and snowy weather, it may be caused by water ingress into the electrical parts. In this case, check whether the vehicle body leaks by spraying water on the vehicle (similar to washing the vehicle).

    • Load test

      1. If a fault occurs only when electrical equipment is turned on, turn on all electrical appliances (including A/C, audio system and fog lights) in sequence, determine the relationship between each electrical appliance and the fault, and confirm the fault by a load test.

    • Voltage drop test

      1. Usually, the voltage drop test is used to identify the causes that may affect the normal operation of electrical parts or circuits.

      2. A multimeter is used to check the circuit.

      3. The wiring harness or circuit is determined as normal, if the resistance of a single wiring harness measured by a multimeter is very small (0 Ω or close to 0 Ω) and the voltage drop test result is normal.
