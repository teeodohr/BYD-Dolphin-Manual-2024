System descriptions

Overview

There are active and passive safety systems in vehicles. Active safety means the ability to identify dangers in advance (e.g., windshield vision, lighting system, mirrors, etc.) and to avoid dangers in advance (e.g., steering system, brake system, driving antiskid system, and vehicle body stability control system). Passive safety means to avoid or reduce the harm to the occupants, such as seat belts and airbags.

The supplemental restraint system (SRS) is a passive and deployable supplemental protective system. A vehicle equipped with this system can be quickly identified by the SRS or AIRBAG mark.

The driver airbag mark is cast on the trim cover in the middle of the steering wheel, while the passenger airbag mark is cast on the instrument panel. A vehicle equipped with SRS can also be identified by the SRS MIL on the instrument panel cluster. Each time when the power supply is turned to the ON position for system self-test, the SRS MIL on the instrument panel cluster will illuminate for about 3s.

Composition of SRS:

  • Front collision sensor I

  • Side collision sensor

  • SRS MIL

  • SRS electronic control unit

  • Clock spring

  • Driver airbag module

  • Front passenger airbag module

  • Side curtain airbag module

  • Seat belt reminder sensor

The SRS ECU is installed on the central axis of the vehicle, below the auxiliary instrument panel body. If the voltage of the 12 V battery is too low or the power supply is cut off due to collision, the SRS ECU can still continue to work for more than 100 ms.

The driver airbag is installed in the steering wheel central trim cover and cannot be disassembled. It consists of gas generator, air bag, trim cover, bracket and other auxiliaries. The function of the driver airbag is to receive ignition signal from the SRS ECU and ignite the gas generator to generate a large amount of gas to inflate the air bag and form an air cushion for protecting the driver.

The clock spring is installed on the multi-function switch and composed of spiral cable, rotor, shell, wiring harness and auxiliary structural parts. When the steering wheel is turned, the rotor and steering wheel rotate as a whole and the spiral cable with sufficient length is coiled in the shell spirally so that it will not affect the reliable connection of the wire when the rotor rotates by 2.5 turns clockwise or counterclockwise from the middle position.

The front passenger airbag module is installed inside the instrument panel and above the instrument panel body, with the same function as that of the driver airbag module.

The front left and right collision sensors are installed inside the left and right water tank pillars. They are designed to transmit a collision signal to the SRS ECU as the basis for the SRS ECU to determine whether an ignition signal or a collision unlocking signal needs to be sent.

Airbag wiring harness are used to connect SRS ECU, driver airbag module, front passenger airbag module, side curtain airbag modules, clock spring and instrument panel wiring harness. The airbag wiring harness consists of airbag module drive circuit, MIL circuit, collision unlocking circuit and vehicle communication bus. They are used to communicate signals between the SRS ECU and the airbag module as well as the vehicle and to ensure reliable communication. The airbag circuit wiring harness can be identified by its special color (yellow) cable or plug.

The SRS MIL is located on the instrument panel cluster and will illuminate for informing the driver of a fault in the SRS in case of any fault identified by the SRS ECU self-diagnosis circuit. Under normal circumstances, when the vehicle is powered ON, the MIL will illuminate for about 3s and then go out.

Components that assist the SRS:

Lower left shield of instrument panel: It is a molded structural part located at the rear side of the steering column opening cover and integrated with the instrument panel to protect the driver's knee from collision.

Seat belt: In case of emergency braking, collision or rollover, it firmly restrains the driver and the passenger on the seats to limit their position, so as to avoid or reduce the damage to them caused by the secondary collision due to inertia force.

Seat belt buckle with reminder assembly: It monitors whether the driver and passengers fasten seat belts.

Child safety lock: When provided, the rear passengers cannot open the doors in the vehicle, thus avoiding the danger of accidental opening by children and sudden opening during vehicle collision.

Anti-collision energy-absorbing vehicle body and accessories: They include bumper assembly, door assembly and bumper beam.

The working of airbag depends on the angle and severity of vehicle collision. The collision angle specified to activate the SRS is within 30° in front of the body centerline. The deployment of an airbag does not depend on the vehicle speed but on the deceleration ratio measured by gravity (KG) and this force is measured by the collision sensor in SRS ECU. When a frontal collision is serious enough, the microprocessor in the SRS ECU will send a working signal to the deployment device of the 2 airbag modules to make the airbag deploy. The clock spring at the top of the steering column allows a continuous circuit to be maintained between the fixed steering column and the driver airbag expander, which can also rotate with the steering wheel. When the vehicle suffers a frontal collision and the airbags deploy, the lower left shield of the instrument panel works together with the seat belts to restrain the driver and front passenger in place. The lower left shield of the instrument panel can also absorb and disperse the impact energy of the driver and front passenger acting on the instrument panel structure. When the SRS ECU detects a fault in any airbag component or SRS circuit, it stores a corresponding DTC in its memory and sends a message to the instrument panel cluster to illuminate the SRS MIL. The VDS is needed to properly test the SRS components, read or clear DTCs, and perform maintenance.
