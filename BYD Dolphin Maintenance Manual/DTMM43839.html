Emergency Handling

First Aid

The workshop must be staffed with first aid training.

Some examples of immediate action to be taken in case of emergency are given below:

  • If foreign objects splash into the eyes, carefully clean them with clean water for at least 10 min.

  • Skin contaminated with the material must be washed with soap and water.

  • If the body is burned by some substances, it must be washed with cold water immediately to cool down.

  • If one inhales fumes, he must immediately be moved to a place with fresh air and his physical condition is observed. If the situation still does not be improved, the patient must be taken to hospital immediately.

  • If person accidentally swallows the liquid, the information contained in the container or label must be provided to the doctor for consultation. Do not attempt vomiting on your own unless otherwise indicated on the label.

  • In case of accidental electric shock, turn off the power supply and organize rescue for the sake of your safety.

Emergency Definition

Definition: An emergency refers to an abnormal fault that occurs suddenly during the normal operation of the vehicle, with no predictability and preparation in advance.

Category:

  • Common faults that do not affect the passengers and drivers and property safety.

  • Serious faults affecting the passengers and drivers and property safety.

Emergency response principles:

  • Handling of common faults: For common faults, you can go to the service station for normal vehicle maintenance.

  • Handling of serious faults: The handling of serious faults is based on the principle of "Life First", that is, in any emergency, the safety of passengers and drivers will be guaranteed first.

Collision Handling

Vehicle power battery leakage:

  1. Set the vehicle to OFF and keep it stationary for 5min.

  2. Power off the low voltage electrical system. See  

  3. Disconnect the power maintenance switch (if equipped);

  4. Disconnect the positive and negative busbars of the power battery;

  5. Insulate and seal the positive and negative busbar connectors of the power battery and the harness connectors to prevent short circuits and foreign objects.

  6. In case of a small amount of electrolyte leakage, keep away from the fire source, absorb the electrolyte with an absorbent pad and place it in a closed container, or dispose it by incineration; In case of a large number of leaks, collect them uniformly and treat them as hazardous chemicals. Calcium gluconate solution can be added for treatment to reduce the generation of harmful gas HF.

  7. Drag the vehicle to the service store for power battery removal, and store the power battery safely after removal.

  8. For the above steps, see Safe Maintenance  and Battery Safety  conduct the whole process monitoring and risk identification and treatment.

    Operators in steps 3, 4, and 5, and e must wear insulating shoes, insulating gloves and insulating pads.

    Operators in steps 6 and 7 must wear insulating shoes, acid and alkali resistant gloves and protective goggles.

Deformation of power battery:

  1. Set the vehicle to OFF and keep it stationary for 5min.

  2. Power off the low voltage electrical system. See  

  3. Disconnect the maintenance switch (if equipped).

  4. Disconnect the positive and negative busbars of the power battery;

  5. Use insulating tape to insulate and seal the positive and negative busbar connectors of the power battery and the harness connectors to prevent short circuits and foreign objects.

  6. Drag the vehicle to the service store for power battery removal, and store the power battery safely after removal.

  7. Disconnect each module of the power battery, if seriously deformed, for storage;

  8. For the above steps, see Safe Maintenance  and Battery Safety  conduct the whole process monitoring and risk identification and treatment.

    Operators in steps 3, 4, 5, 6, and 7, and e must wear insulating shoes, insulating gloves and insulating pads.

Damaged vehicle sealing:

  1. When waiting for maintenance, move the vehicle to a place without water ingress and corrosion risk for safe storage;

  2. If the vehicle cannot be moved to a place without water ingress and corrosion risk for safe storage, it is necessary to cover it with a waterproof cover to avoid the risk of water ingress and corrosion.

Smoke and Fire

Fire response plan

  1. Use non-water-based fire extinguishers and fire-fighting sand to extinguish fires in non-power battery parts and when the fire is small;

  2. In the case that the above methods cannot realize the fire fighting, a large amount of water can be used to soak the vehicle;

  3. After the fire in the power battery is put out, the power battery must be continuously cleaned with water for 30 min.

Reminder

  • In a fire, irritating toxic gases will be generated due to combustion or thermal decomposition reaction. In any closed area, firefighters must wear full protective equipment including positive pressure self-contained breathing apparatus;

  • Do not open or remove the control module cover with the high voltage identification. Otherwise, serious electrical fire or electric shock may occur.

Flooded Vehicle

Maintenance response plan

High voltage components of the vehicle have not been soaked in water:

  1. Test whether the vehicle reports electric leakage with VDS.

  2. If no electric leakage is reported, perform the routine maintenance.

  3. If the immersion height reaches the threshold for more than 30 min or leakage fault has been reported, see Safe Maintenance and Battery Safety  carry out maintenance.

High voltage components of the vehicle have been soaked in water:

  1. Set the vehicle to OFF and keep it stationary for 5min.

  2. Power off the low voltage electrical system. See  

  3. Disconnect the maintenance switch (if equipped).

  4. Disconnect the positive and negative busbars of the power battery;

  5. Use insulating tape to insulate and seal the positive and negative busbar connectors of the power battery and the harness connectors to prevent short circuits and foreign objects.

  6. Transfer the vehicle to the service store;

  7. See Safe Maintenance  Verify whether the power battery has electric leakage.

  8. If the power battery does not have electric leakage, refer to safe maintenance and the service manual for maintenance.

  9. If the vehicle has been immersed in water for more than 30 min or the power battery has electric leakage, remove the power battery. After removal, place the power battery safely, and disconnect each module of the power battery.

    Note: Operators in steps 3, 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 must wear insulating shoes, insulating gloves and insulating pads.

Parts above the power battery pack bus bar are soaked in water:

  1. Set the vehicle to OFF and keep it stationary for 5min.

  2. Power off the low voltage electrical system. See  

  3. Disconnect the maintenance switch (if equipped).

  4. Disconnect the positive and negative busbars of the power battery;

  5. Use insulating tape to insulate and seal the positive and negative busbar connectors of the power battery and the harness connectors to prevent short circuits and foreign objects.

  6. Take the vehicle to the service store. After removal, place the power battery safely, and disconnect each module of the power battery.

For the operation specifications of the above steps see Battery Safety . Meanwhile, risk monitoring must be carried out.

Emergency Treatment of Personnel Injury

Determination of electrolyte leakage of power battery:

  1. In case of a large amount of electrolyte leakage, it is visible by naked eyes;

  2. In case of a small amount of electrolyte leakage, such areas will be wet or have milky white spots;

  3. Pungent smell means there is electrolyte leakage;

Emergency treatment method for exposure to electrolyte:

  • Under normal circumstances, when some human body parts are accidentally exposed to the leaked electrolyte, rinse the body parts with plenty of water for 10~15 min immediately. If there is pain, apply 2.5% calcium gluconate ointment to the pain areas or soak such areas in 2~2.5% calcium gluconate solution to relieve pain. If no improvement or discomfort occurs, seek medical advice immediately.

  • In case of eye contact, rinse eyes with fresh water for at least 15 min. If there is still a pain feeling, please seek medical advice immediately.

  • In case of skin contact, remove contaminated clothing, wipe contaminants with a clean cloth immediately, and then rinse the contaminated areas thoroughly with soap and water. If there is still a pain feeling, please seek medical advice immediately.

  • In case of respiratory tract contact, move to a fresh air place. If you still breathe hard or feel unwell, please seek medical advice immediately. In case of apnea, take cardiopulmonary resuscitation measures and seek medical advice immediately.

Emergency treatment method for electric shock:

  1. Turn off the power immediately if you can ensure your own safety.

  2. Take insulation protection measures for yourself and others, and separate electric shock personnel from power supply.

  3. To conduct emergency medical assistance on the scene, after the person who gets an electric shock is separated from the power supply, if he/she is conscious, let he/she rest quietly; in case of severe burns, send he/she to a hospital for treatment. If the person is unconscious and still has heartbeat and breathing, make the person lie on the back, and unbutton the clothes to facilitate breathing; ensure the surrounding air is circulated, closely observe him/her, and see a doctor immediately for treatment or send he/she to a hospital for examination and treatment. If the electrocuted person has stopped breathing and the heart has stopped beating but has not died, defibrillators (if any) must be used immediately or cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) must be performed. The operation of cardiopulmonary resuscitation is as follows:

    1. Lay the electrocuted person on his back on a board or hard ground.

    2. Unbutton the neckline and trouser belt, and make his/her head tilt back as far as possible with nostrils facing the sky, so that the base of his/her tongue will not block the airway.

    3. Then open his mouth by hand to clean the foreign matters in the mouth and make the airway unblocked.

    4. Hold up his/her jaw with one hand, pinch his/her nose with the other hand, and give artificial respiration for about 2 s to expand the chest of the rescued person; then release his/her mouth and nose to let his/her chest compress naturally for exhalation about 3s.

    5. Repeat this procedure, with air blew about 12 times per minute.

    6. If the mouth of the person who gets an electric shock can't be opened, give mouth-to-nose artificial respiration instead. At this time, larger blowing pressure is required and the air blowing time must be slightly longer, so as to facilitate the air to enter the lungs.

    7. If the person who gets an electric shock is a child, blow a small amount of air to avoid damaging his/her lungs.

  4. Chest compressions must be performed at the same time of artificial respiration, about 100 compressions per minute with a compression depth of 5 cm.
