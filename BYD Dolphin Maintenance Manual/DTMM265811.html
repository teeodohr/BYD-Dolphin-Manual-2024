System descriptions

Overview

The IPB system includes a brake booster and a brake adjustment module, which replaces the traditional EVP+ESC module, greatly reducing the weight of the whole system. The decoupled brake system is used to solve the problem of vehicle brake feedback.

The IPB system consists of the following functions:

The anti-lock brake system (ABS) can control the brakeforce in a real-time manner during emergency braking to prevent deviation and sideslip, so that the vehicle can always maintain good directional stability and operability, and the safety of vehicle braking can be improved.

The electronic brakeforce distribution (EBD) system is used to balance the effective grip force of each wheel before ABS action in case that the wheels are locked during emergency braking. This can prevent drifting and side shifting and shorten the braking distance.

The traction control system (TCS) enables the vehicle to obtain the best traction under various driving conditions. The system receives the driver's steering intention through the steering wheel angle sensor, and then detects the speed difference between the left and right wheels with the left and right wheel speed sensors, so as to judge whether the vehicle steering conforms to the driver's steering intention. If understeer (or oversteer) is detected, the system immediately judges that the drive force of the drive wheel is too large, and issues an instruction to reduce the drive force, so as to realize the driver's steering intention.

The hill-start hold control (HHC) system is derived from the ESP system. It allows the vehicle to keep braking for seconds when the driver's right foot leaves the brake pedal if the vehicle starts on a hill when the handbrake is not applicable, so that the driver can easily move his/her foot from the brake pedal to the accelerator pedal, and accidents due to sliding can be avoided. Therefore, the system makes the driver operate more easily.

The hydraulic brake assist (HBA) system can help the driver to apply emergency braking. It judges whether there is a need for full braking based on the speed at which the driver depresses the brake pedal. As long as the driver keeps depressing the pedal to the floor, the system will automatically increase the brakeforce until the threshold of ABS starting is reached. If the driver releases the brake pedal, the system will reduce the brakeforce to the specified value.

Controller deceleration parking (CDP): Nowadays, the vehicle is generally equipped with the EPB instead of traditional handbrake, and there is an EPB switch on the vehicle. The CDP can enable the EPB switch to act as a traditional handbrake (the EPB switch can be pulled to brake the vehicle dynamically when the vehicle is running).

The vehicle dynamic control (VDC) system is used to improve the driving stability, but it cannot prevent accidents caused by rapid sharp turns or careless or dangerous driving operations.

The automatic vehicle hold (AVH) system has the function of automatically pulling the handbrake. After this function is activated, for example, when the vehicle stops at traffic lights, the driver does not need to pull the handbrake. This function is especially suitable for going up and down hills and frequently starting and stopping the vehicle.

The electronic stability system (ESS) is a braking system that prevents the vehicle from slipping under emergency driving conditions. Activeness is one of its main characteristics. Unlike the ABS which reacts passively, it can nip trouble in the bud.
