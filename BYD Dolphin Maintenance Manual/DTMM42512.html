Systems Manual

Overview

The wheel assembly is composed of aluminum alloy wheel and tire.

Correct tire pressure, wheel alignment and driving skills have a significant impact on tire life.

Driving habits such as sharp cornering, rapidly accelerating and hard braking can accelerate the wear of the tires.

Structure of Tire

  1. Belted layer

    The steel cord between the tread and the carcass protects the carcass, suppresses the deformation of the tread, maintains the ground of the tread, and improves the wear resistance and driving stability.

  2. Crown layer

    The special cord ply above the belt layer can restrain the movement of the belt ply during tire driving, prevent the belt ply from separating at high speed, and keep the tire dimensional stability at high speed.

  3. Tread

    The part of the tire in contact with the road surface must have the driving and braking performance through friction, and must have good wear resistance, puncture resistance, impact resistance, heat dissipation and other performance.

  4. Tire

    The cord ply in the tire is the main stressed component in the tire, which is impact resistant with good flex resistance during driving.

  5. Bead

    The tape-hanging wire is wound according to a certain shape (quadrilateral or hexagonal shape) to install the tire onto the rim fixing tire.

  6. Air impervious liner

    The airtight part of the inner tube-free tire is made of special rubber, which is equivalent to the function of the inner tube.

  7. Bead filler strip

    The padding on the bead in the tire prevents bead dispersion, slows bead impact, protects the bead and prevents air from entering during molding.

Meaning of tire pressure and description of air pressure

Tire pressure has a decisive influence on the wear and tear of the tire, so it is necessary to maintain the standard pressure and check the pressure regularly for safe driving.

The load capacity of the tire corresponds to its inflation pressure, and the reasonable air pressure of the tire must be determined according to the load of the vehicle. Changes in climate and seasons must not be a reason to adjust tire pressure.

In the early stage of the new tire, the outer rim size of the tire will change due to the heat of flexion movement, which will reduce the tire pressure. Therefore, the air pressure must be checked and adjusted after 24 hours of use or driving 2000-3000 km.

The tire pressure must be increased by 10%-15% for driving with a high speed for a long time.

Hazards of insufficient air pressure:

Insufficient air pressure will cause tire sidewall deformation and increase heat generation, greatly reducing tire life and leading to the following problems and safety hazards:

  • Excessive wear at the shoulder.

  • Increase the possible bulging caused by tire impact.

  • A loss of adhesion between the various parts of the tire causes the delamination.

  • A loss of adhesion between the various parts of the tire causes the delamination.

  • Seriously insufficient air pressure causes sidewall rolling damage.

  • Excessive tire runout causes abnormal abrasion between bead and rim with damage for rim.

  • The rolling resistance increases and the power consumption increases.

Hazards of excessive air pressure:

Excessive air pressure will reduce the ground area of the tire tread, increase the rigidity of the carcass, and reduce the cushioning performance, and the following problems and potential safety hazards will be caused:

  • Excessive wear at the center of the tread.

  • Increase the risk of tire rupture or even blowout when impacted by external forces.

  • The decrease of grounding area will result in the decrease of controllability and easy to cause drift, sliding and other hazards.

  • Passenger comfort is reduced.

  • Poor ride comfort and long-term running with excessive air pressure may cause damage to the vehicle chassis.

Uneven tire pressure on the same suspension can lead to:

  • The braking force is not evenly left and right.

  • Steering wheel deflection.

  • Reduced maneuverability.

  • Deflection during acceleration.

  • The vehicle deviates during driving.
