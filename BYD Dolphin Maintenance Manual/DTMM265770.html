Precautions

Caution

  • ACC is a system to enhance comfort, rather than a safety system, nor an obstacle detector or collision warning system. The driver must maintain control of the vehicle at all times, and is fully responsible for the vehicle's operation.

  • The ACC function can assist the driver, but it cannot replace the driver in driving. The driver must always maintain control of the vehicle and be fully responsible for the vehicle, following traffic regulations at all times.

  • For safety, ACC cannot be activated when ESC is not turned on.

  • ACC is suitable for use on expressways and roads in good conditions, but unsuitable for use on complex urban roads or mountain roads.

  • Maintaining a proper distance from a vehicle ahead is the responsibility of the driver. The following distance of the ACC system shall meet the minimum distance requirements as stipulated by various countries for the specific driving environment.

  • When the ACC is active, if the driver presses the accelerator pedal or brake pedal, the control of the vehicle will be handed over to the driver, and the ACC system will no longer keep a safe distance from the vehicle ahead.

  • If a vehicle ahead brakes suddenly (emergency stop), ACC may fail to react or be slow in reacting to the vehicle ahead, resulting in the risk of late braking. In this case, the driver does not receive a take-over request.

  • In some cases (a vehicle ahead running slowly, the vehicle changing lanes too suddenly, or keeping excessively short safety distance), ACC may not have enough time to reduce the relative speed. In such cases, the driver must react appropriately. ACC cannot provide an audio or visual alarm in each case.

  • When ACC is activated while the vehicle is stationary, it may identify any stationary obstacle in front as a vehicle, so it may then keep the vehicle stationary to ensure a safe starting and avoid collision with the stationary target caused by unexpected starting. However, this function cannot identify all obstacles, so the driver must ensure that there are no obstacles or other traffic participants in front of the vehicle.

  • If the vehicle with ACC activated runs too close to an adjacent lane (or another vehicle on an adjacent lane runs too close to the lane on which the vehicle with ACC activated is running), ACC may apply braking in response to the corresponding vehicle.

  • If another vehicle changes to the lane on which the vehicle with ACC activated is running and falls within the detection range of the front mmWave radar, it may be identified as a target vehicle, and a response may be made correspondingly. In this event, forced braking or late braking may occur.

  • In some circumstances, detection may be affected or delayed. If the front mmWave radar reflection cross-sectional area of the target is too small (e.g., a bicycle, carriage, or pedestrian), there is a risk that ACC may be unable to identify the distance from the target ahead, resulting in late response or no response to these targets. In such cases, the driver needs to control the vehicle speed. Moreover, the detection may be affected by noise or electromagnetic interference, resulting in delay or interference.

  • When the overlap between the vehicle and the vehicle ahead is too small, the ACC system may be unable to identify the vehicle ahead as the target. In this event, the driver needs to keep control of the vehicle.

  • When the vehicle follows a vehicle ahead to stop, ACC is unable to identify the end of the target vehicle ahead and instead identifies its lower end (e.g. the rear axle of a truck with high chassis or the bumper of a vehicle) in very few cases. Should these occur, ACC is unable to ensure a proper stopping distance, and the driver must stay vigilant and ready to brake at any time.

  • Structural retrofitting of the vehicle, such as lowering the chassis or changing the front license plate frame, may compromise the ACC system.

  • Do not use the ACC system when driving on roads with poor visibility, ramps, or multiple curves, or on slippery and wet roads with snow, ice, or water gathered.

  • In the following situations, professional calibration and checking of the front mmWave radar and multi-purpose camera must be performed by a BYD authorized dealer or service provider:

    1. Remove the front mmWave radar, front bumper and front windshield.

    2. Four wheels have been re-aligned due to wheel deviation;

    3. A collision happens;

    4. The ACC system performance degrades or the instrument panel cluster indicates a system abnormality.
