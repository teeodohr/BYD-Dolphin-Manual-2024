Analysis of coating defects

Overview of paint film defects

Classification of paint film defects

Reminder
Most defects of paints are formed during operations. Some coating defects are repairable during operations, while the other ones may be repaired after being dried only.

Defects generated during paint application

  1. Key points

    • Paint film defects caused by paint or spraying operation: dust spots, polishing marks, sagging, blistering, fish eyes, coating shrinkage, uneven aluminum powder, cracking, uneven aluminum powder reflux, fogging, orange peel, paint peeling, solvent bubble, bleeding, pinhole, color difference, sandpaper mark, transition black circles of aluminum powder, gloss loss, poly-putty marks, etc.

Paint film defects caused by external factors

  1. Key points

    • Paint film defects caused by external factors: water marks, pollen spots, acid rain spots, black spots, bird droppings, iron dust, oil spots, battery liquid spots, flying stone damage, fading or whitening (pulverization), ash spots and rust, etc.

Painting defect inspection

  1. Key points

    • Check the whole vehicle.

    • Observe the surrounding environment where the vehicle was originally parked.

    • Observe the paint film defects and identify their shape, size and color.

    • Check the surrounding surface for irregularities.

    • Observe each defect and compare the examples of defects.

Conditions for paint film defect inspection

  1. Key points

    • Check the paint film surface from different perspectives.

  2. Operation steps

    • Direct view

    • Side view

    • Use light sources appropriately

    • Sunlight

    • In the shade

    • Fluorescent lamp

Treatment method for paint film defects

  1. Key points

    • Heating: The deformation of the paint film surface caused by external force can be repaired by heating. After cleaning the vehicle, heat the paint film surface with an infrared heating lamp.

    • Stain removal: Stains caused by grease and dirty substances can be removed from the paint film surface with solvent.

    • Polishing: For the paint film dissolved or with concaves or particles, it can be polished to restore the surface gloss. If necessary, it can also be ground before polishing.

    • Repaint: If the defects cannot be removed by heating or polishing, grind the paint film surface until the defects are completely removed before repainting.

Defects generated during coating application

Fish eyes

  1. Key points

    • Fish eyes are small crater-like silica pits dotted in the surface of wet coating. Sometimes, substrate can be seen at the bottom of the pits.

  2. Causes

    • The vehicle is not completely degreased.

    • Dirty cloth is used for degreasing.

    • The compressed air contains water or oil.

    • The spray booth is contaminated by wax, silicon and other chemicals. Common materials containing silicon and wax in sheet metal painting include surface plate wax, bolt loosening agent and body paint maintenance wax.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Thoroughly degrease the vehicle before spraying. When degreaser is used, use two pieces of cloth for wiping in turn.

    • Check the oil-water separator and drain it if necessary.

    • Do not use products containing silicon and silica in the painting workshop or spray booth, and use water-soluble polishing wax without silicon wax for polishing.

  4. Solutions

    • Thoroughly degrease the coating with fish eyes.

    • Grind the coating with fish eyes flat.

    • Spray a thin layer first, and then spray it normally. Volatilization time between coatings should be considered as required.

    • If fish eyes are still formed in the coating, add anti-fish eye solution and continue spraying.

Dust spots

  1. Key points

    • They are dust particles falling on the wet coating and embedded in the paint film when the paint is dry.

  2. Causes

    • During spraying, dust flies from the sunroof, wheel arch cover or gap.

    • Dust particles remain on the vehicle surface and paint is contaminated.

    • Plastic parts have static electricity and absorb dust.

    • Dust flies for movement or walking around the vehicle.

    • Factors of spray booth: blocked filter cotton, dust on the floor, dirty walls, and the top surface requiring to be replaced.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Thoroughly clean all openings around the repair area.

    • Mask the area required to be masked with the masking paper by folding its tear edge inward and facing its smooth side outward.

    • Filter the paint when it is poured into the spray gun.

    • Wear clean, fiber-free and anti-static paint workwear (nylon).

    • Treat with anti-static agent, and do not degrease the plastic parts afterwards.

    • Maintain the spray booth and replace filter materials regularly.

    • Wipe the 2m-long air pipe of the spray gun with an old dust cloth, and hang up the air pipe after spraying.

  4. Solutions

    • During spraying, the needle tip can be used to remove dust in the paint film.

    • Small dust in the dry coating can be removed by polishing.

    • If dust is trapped deep in the coating, the surface should be ground and repainted.

Orange peel

  1. Key points

    • The new sprayed paint has poor leveling performance and looks like an orange peel.

  2. Causes

    • Too high spraying viscosity

    • Too high drying speed of thinner

    • Too high or too low spraying pressure, and too large nozzle

    • Too high or too low ambient temperature

    • Excessive anti-fish eye solution added to the paint to be used

  3. Preventive measures

    • Mix in the correct proportions with a scale and check viscosity.

    • Select the thinner with the following factors considered: ambient temperature, workpiece size and airflow rate. Please refer to the technical information manual.

    • Keep an ideal ambient temperature for spraying at about 20 °C.

    • Properly use the anti-fish eye solution to ensure a correct mixing proportion.

  4. Solutions

    • Slight orange peel can be removed by polishing. If it is serious, the surface must be ground and repainted.

Sagging

  1. Key points

    • Sagging occurs in some areas with uneven coating thickness, mainly on vertical surfaces and occurs in local areas with too much paint even when the paint is wet.

  2. Causes

    • The substrate is not thoroughly degreased so that the paint cannot adhere to the substrate firmly.

    • The drying speed of the selected thinner is too slow, and too much thinner is added to the paint.

    • Paint builds up in some areas due to too short spraying distance or uneven spraying.

    • The ambient temperature is too low and the thinner volatilizes too slowly.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Select the thinner with the following factors considered: workpiece size, ambient temperature and airflow rate.

    • Use the correct spraying technique.

  4. Solutions

    • The dried sagging paint can be removed by grinding and polishing.

    • In case of severe sagging, it must be ground off with the surface repainted after drying.

Whitening and frosting

  1. Key points

    • It refers to whitening or fogging of the newly sprayed coating.

  2. Causes

    • The quick-drying thinner is used to sharply cool the sprayed surface, resulting in immediate condensation of water vapor on the wet coating surface.

    • Excessive spraying pressure causes the workpiece to cool down, followed by moisture condensation.

    • The sprayed plate is not completely dried before being put into use, and condensate invades the coating.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Use slow-drying thinner in wet weather.

    • Avoid spraying in a wet and cold environment.

    • Regularly check the airflow rate of spray booth.

    • Select the corresponding drying time according to the workpiece temperature.

  4. Solutions

    • Polish the repair area if the problem is not serious, or remove the top coat by grinding and repaint the surface if it is serious.

Spotting and clouding

  1. Key points

    • Clouding only occurs in metallic primer. Uneven aluminum pigment in the primer causes uneven color, like clouding or spotting.

  2. Causes

    • Thinner: Quick-drying thinner is used.

    • Spraying: Incorrect spray gun setting, too heavy primer, wrong pressure and spraying distance.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Select the thinner based on ambient temperature, repair area and airflow rate.

    • Use the recommended settings for spray guns.

    • Spray primer according to the technical data guide.

  4. Solutions

    • When primer spraying is still under way, respray it according to the recommended procedure or spraying technique.

    • After the varnish is sprayed, sand it with P500 dry grinder or P1000 wet sandpaper, and then respray according to the precautions mentioned above.

Pinhole

  1. Key points

    • It refers to small holes on the surface of the paint film, which cannot be filled by the outer paint film and form dents in the surface.

  2. Causes

    • Poor quality of the selected thinner

    • Incorrect spraying pressure or improper nozzle size

    • Too thick coating and too short interval between coatings

    • No volatilization time given

    • Manual drying immediately after spraying, and too high drying temperature

    • Infrared drying equipment used too early, or too short distance from equipment to vehicle

  3. Preventive measures

    • Select high-quality thinner suitable for ambient temperature.

    • Refer to the technical information manual and select appropriate spraying pressure and nozzle size.

    • Use the correct spraying technique and give sufficient volatilization time.

    • Follow the recommended volatilization time to allow the last layer of paint to volatilize for a period before manual drying.

    • Allow the coating to volatilize normally before infrared drying, and keep a proper distance between the infrared equipment and the vehicle.

  4. Solutions

    • Grind the paint film surface until all solvent bubble marks are removed, and then respray it.

Wrinkling and paint lifting

  1. Key points

    • When the paint is sprayed, the substrate is partially dissolved.

  2. Causes

    • The selected coating does not match the substrate.

    • The adhesion between the previous coating and substrate is poor.

    • It is repainted before the substrate is completely dry or hardened.

    • The paint is sprayed too much at one time.

    • The volatilization time is too long, and the previous volatile coating is dissolved by the solvent of the next layer of paint.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Analyze the substrate of the repair area and select a suitable primer for the substrate, adopt the correct mixing proportion and use appropriate spraying technique to prevent excessive thickness.

    • Apply the next coating after a suitable volatilization time.

  4. Solutions

    • To some extent, the lifted paint can be ground to a sound paint layer after it is completely dry. Then, repaint as required. For more sensitive substrates, paint carefully: the coating should be thin, and sufficient volatilization time is required.

    • In case of serious paint lifting, it must be completely removed before repainting.

Gloss loss

  1. Causes

    • The wet coating absorbs wax or other similar contaminants.

    • The poly-putty is not completely dry before grinding.

    • The grinding sandpaper is too coarse, and the drying speed of selected thinner is too high.

    • The drying agent does not match the paint.

    • Improper amount of drying agent or thinner is added.

    • The mixing method of each component is incorrect (two-component product).

    • The baking temperature is too high or the baking time is too long.

  2. Preventive measures

    • Thoroughly degrease the repair area and its surroundings before grinding and spraying.

    • Set the drying time according to ambient temperature and paint film thickness.

    • Select appropriate sandpaper with reference to the product-related technical manual.

    • Select the drying agent recommended by the paint manufacturer and compatible with the thinner.

    • Ensure a correct mixing proportion with the help of a scale.

    • Follow the recommended volatilization time and prevent excessive spraying from causing excessive coating thickness.

    • Select the appropriate drying time before polishing according to the instructions in the product technical manual.

  3. Solutions

    • Polish to increase gloss. If it cannot be repaired, grind it before repainting.

Sandpaper marks

  1. Key points

    • Fine scratches can be found on the paint film, mostly on the top coat. This problem may be identified soon or after several weeks. We can identify the grinding shape of a grinder or manual grinding pad.

  2. Causes

    • The selected sandpaper is too coarse, leaving coarse sandpaper marks after grinding the repair area.

    • The primer or poly-putty coating is not completely dry before grinding.

    • Scratches are caused by sand particles or coarse dust particles during grinding.

    • Deep scratches are caused by too coarse sandpaper during manual grinding.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Refer to the technical information manual and select appropriate sandpaper for products in the next process.

    • Use sandpaper with 100 meshes finer than that in the repair area for grinding.

    • Make sure that the substrate coating is sufficiently dry before grinding.

    • Remove all dust by dust removal or blow gun before grinding.

    • Use sandpaper of one grade finer for manual grinding.

  4. Solutions

    • After the paint film is dry, grind the top coat with appropriate sandpaper and repaint if necessary.

Paint film defects caused by external factors

Water marks

  1. Key points

    • It refers to water droplet-like marks on the coating after drying.

  2. Causes

    • Improper amount of drying agent is added.

    • The coating is so thick that it cannot be completely dried within the recommended time.

    • The coating fails to dry properly.

    • The coating contacts with rainwater or water droplets during its cooling.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Mix in the correct proportions with the aid of a scale.

    • Avoid excessive thickness.

    • Dry the coating according to the standard drying time and temperature.

    • Allow the vehicle to cool down before driving out or rinsing with water in rainy days.

  4. Solutions

    • Polish the surface until marks disappear. Grind and repaint the area if polishing does not work or the problem reappears after polishing.

Pulverization

  1. Key points

    • The resin in the paint layer is pulverized for exposure to the ultraviolet rays, and a powder layer forms on the paint surface throughout the discoloration area (mainly a single-process paint system).

  2. Causes

    • Paint products: curing agent not matching the paint, inappropriate or poor-quality curing agent, and affected interlinking reaction between paint resin and curing agent. The sprayed coating is pulverized.

    • Operation and maintenance: pulverization of the paint coating as the vehicle is cleaned with strong-dissolving solvent or cleaning solution.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Use OEM certified paint products.

    • Use high-quality paint.

    • Use high-quality cleaning solution or detergent.

    • Regularly wax the paint surface.

  4. Solutions

    • Slight pulverization can be handled by polishing, while the color can be protected by waxing. In case of repeated pulverization, grind the top coat and repaint as required.

    • In case of severe pulverization, repaint after grinding.

Cracking

  1. Key points

    • After a period of time, widespread fine cracks may appear on the paint surface, and some may even penetrate all paint layers.

  2. Causes

    • Old paint layer: This problem will reappear after some time if the cracked old paint layer is directly painted again.

    • Spraying: unmatched paint coating, two-component paint sprayed on the soft old paint layer, and too thick coating.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Do not spray paint on the cracked old paint layer: remove the whole paint layer to the metal surface, and repaint according to the technical requirements.

    • Avoid incompatible paint, and check the substrate before repair.

    • Follow the technical requirements.

  4. Solutions

    • The only way is to completely remove the cracked paint layer to the metal surface before repainting.

Poor coating hardening

  1. Key points

    • After a long period of time, the paint is still not completely dry, and fingerprints will easily appear on the surface when pressed hard.

  2. Causes

    • Hardener selection error: incorrect hardener used, not dry sufficiently, and hardener expired or reacted with moisture in the air.

    • Drying: too low drying temperature or too short drying time resulting in insufficient drying of coating, and insufficient air flow in the spray booth

  3. Preventive measures

    • Use a hardener suitable for the ambient temperature.

    • Mix according to the technical data description.

    • Note the service life of hardener or tighten the cover after use.

    • Store the paint product at a suitable temperature.

    • Check the filter and maintain the spray booth regularly.

  4. Solutions

    • Dry the plate for a long time at the temperature stated in the product manual.

    • If the wrong product is used, remove the paint with thinner or by grinding and repaint as required.

Poor adhesion

  1. Key points

    • Poor adhesion appears immediately after spraying and drying the paint, but may also occur several weeks later.

  2. Causes

    • Selection error: mismatched products are used.

    • Degreasing error: Incorrect and poor-quality degreasing product is used. The release agent on new bumper is not removed (new component).

    • Grinding: insufficient grinding or incorrect sandpaper grade and material.

    • Primer: failure to use the matching plastic primer, resulting in unqualified paint coating.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Use OEM certified supporting products.

    • Thoroughly clean the plate surface with recommended degreaser and two pieces of wiping cloth.

    • Grind with sandpaper of an appropriate grade.

    • Select a suitable primer according to the type of substrate.

  4. Solutions

    • Remove the entire paint system until it is smooth. If necessary, remove the entire paint system and prepare and paint as recommended.

Blister

  1. Key points

    • It refers to small bumps scattered or concentrated on the coating surface. Blisters are bubbles resulting from subsequent lifting of the existing paint film from the underlying surface due to water vapor or dirt under the paint after a vehicle is used for a period of time.

  2. Causes

    • Dirt remains on the substrate before spraying.

    • Water vapor condenses on the vehicle after degreasing.

    • Calcium and salt deposits in the grinding water are still left on the vehicle and absorb moisture.

    • The polyester poly-putty has been treated by water grinding or absorbed the moisture in the air.

    • Inappropriate drying agent or thinner is used.

    • The drying agent has reacted with water vapor in the tank.

    • The compressed air contains oil or water.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Check the substrate carefully before and during degreasing.

    • Dry grind the polyester poly-putty.

    • Select the recommended drying agent and thinner.

    • Cover the drying agent tank immediately after use.

    • Check the oil-water separator and drain it regularly as required.

  4. Solutions

    • Remove the defective coating and repaint as required.

Peeling

  1. Key points

    • The top coat, varnish or the whole paint layer peels off. After spraying, peeling may occur directly upon removal of the masking tape or in several weeks and months.

  2. Causes

    • Grinding: insufficient grinding on the plate substrate.

    • Degreaser: insufficient degreasing and poor-quality degreaser.

    • Selection of thinner: Quick-drying thinner will build up moisture on the primer surface in wet weather, and this thin layer of water mist will cause poor adhesion.

    • Application: The primer is too thin and rough, and no tack cloth is used between the paint layers and in the last layer.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Improve the grinding quality before spraying top coat.

    • Use only suitable, quality-assured and correct degreaser.

    • Select the thinner based on ambient temperature, repair area and airflow rate.

    • Spray primer according to the requirements of technical data.

  4. Solutions

    • Remove the paint system to the metal surface and grind out feather edges. If necessary, apply poly-putty and repaint.

Poly-putty marks

  1. Key points

    • It refers to ring-shaped bulges of top coat around the repaired part.

  2. Causes

    • The substrate is not completely hardened so that it absorbs the top coat.

    • The sandpaper is too coarse, and improper thinner is used for the top coat.

    • The parts repaired with poly-putty or filler putty are not properly primed or sealed before spraying of the top coat.

  3. Preventive measures

    • Grind the repaired part until the metal layer is exposed.

    • At the edge of exposed paint layer, use thinner for solvent test. If the paint is softened, this layer must be sealed. Poly-putty and filler putty can only be used on exposed metal instead of covering the original paint surface.

    • Select suitable sandpaper and make sure that all preparatory materials are completely dry.

  4. Solutions

    • After the repair material is dry, grind flat the damaged part and then separate it with priming material before repainting.
