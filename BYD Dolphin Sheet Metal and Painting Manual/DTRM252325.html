Hazards in painting operation

Safety rules for painting workshops

Reminder

  • Those with chronic lung disease or respiratory system problems shall avoid contact with paints and the like.

  • Personnel with skin and respiratory diseases shall avoid contact with paint mist, solvents and volatile gases. Never eat, drink or smoke in the workshop.

  1. Key points

    • All paint products in the painting workshop shall be properly stored and kept away from children.

    • All products must be used in a well-ventilated environment and the operation room with an exhaust system.

    • Automotive repair paint is only used for professional spraying operations or industrial spraying operations.

    • Please consult the authorized service provider or the paint manufacturer for the relevant product instructions and safety rules.

    • Before using any product, it is necessary to carefully read the relevant information and instructions.

Hazards in resin paint

  1. Key points

    • Resin is a synthetic substance.

    • Synthetic resin can cause respiratory allergies and skin allergies.

    • The curing agent of two-component paint may contain isocyanate.

    • Isocyanate can irritate the skin and mucous membranes and cause respiratory disorders.

Hazards of heavy metal

  1. Key points

    • Pigments may contain heavy metals such as lead, chromium, cadmium and iron.

    • Lead can cause harm to the nervous system, blood system, kidney system, and reproductive system.

    • Chromium can harm the respiratory system and digestive tract and cause skin ulceration and nasal septum perforation.

    • Cadmium may cause respiratory tract and renal system disorders.

Hazards of organic solvents

  1. Key points

    • Paints and diluents contain toxic organic solvent components.

    • Paints (top coat, intermediate coat, and primer) and curing agents contain isocyanate, which is highly irritating to the mucous membrane of human respiratory system.

    • If organic solvents accumulate in the body for a long time, they can damage the liver.

    Caution

    • Never spill paint or diluent. Use a rear cover to prevent solvent evaporation.

    • In the workplace or other environments, there are 3 key parts of the human body vulnerable to injury, namely: eyes, respiratory organ, and skin.

    • Organic solvents may enter the human body directly or indirectly through respiration, penetration, ingestion, etc.

    • Use organic solvents in a well ventilated environment as far as possible, and always avoid skin contact with organic solvents.

    Reminder

    • If the organic solvent accidentally contacts the skin or splashes into the eyes, wash it with plenty of water immediately.

    • In case of organic solvent poisoning, move the victim to a well ventilated place immediately, and contact the emergency center quickly.

    • Lower the head of the victim, make his/her face upward or take a lateral position, and ensure that his/her temperature does not drop.

    • If a victim is unconscious due to solvent poisoning, remove vomit from his/her mouth.

    • If a victim stops breathing due to poisoning, conduct artificial respiration immediately.

Hazard of organic solvents

  1. Key points

    • The organic solvent vapor is usually heavier than air, so it tends to sink and remain at the bottom.

    • Organic solvents are commonly regarded as hazardous substances. Therefore, care should be taken when handling and storing organic solvents.

    • Install ventilation devices in low places such as the floor and maintain good ventilation.

    • Be sure to close the lid of a container containing organic solvents.

    • Be sure to keep away from flammable materials and connect the ground wire.

    Caution

    • It is dangerous to work near the floor because it is easy to inhale organic solvent vapor.

    • Open flames, fireworks, smoking, or static electricity may cause explosion or fire.

Dust

  1. Key points

    • Different parts of the human respiratory system have different tolerance to dust.

    • Dust can be easily discharged from the body even if it is inhaled. Inhaled dust may adhere to the nose or throat and be expelled with sputum.

    • The greatest danger is posed by dust particles that easily adhere to the walls of the lungs, measuring between 0.1 and 5 microns.

  2. Operation points

    • During the polishing operation of coating operation, dust masks must be worn correctly.

    Caution

    • Long term accumulation of dust in human body may cause pneumonia or poisoning symptoms.

    Reminder

    • Dust with a size of 0.1-5 µm is most likely to adhere to the lung wall.

    • Usually, dust enters the human body through the respiratory system.

    • µm=0.001 mm.

    • Welding fume: metallic oxides generated by the heating of metal being welded

Heavy objects

  1. Key points

    • When handling heavy objects, squat down to the height of the object and straighten your lower back.

    Caution

    • Bent down to carry heavy objects may cause lower back pain and prone to sprain.

Fire and explosion prevention measures for sheet metal painting workshop

  1. Key points

    • Paints are volatile and inflammable materials, which will cause fire when exposed to flames.

    • When the volatile solvent vapor is mixed with air to a certain concentration during the painting operation, an explosion may occur when the vapor is exposed to an open flame.

    Reminder

    • Before the work, read the instructions for equipment, tools and materials to understand the safe operation matters.

    • Clean the paint and solvent spilled in time.

    • After the work, clean the flammable materials on site and cover the paint buckets.

    • Clean combustibles such as used cotton yarn or rags soaked in solvent in time, put them into metal buckets and make them immersed in clean water.

    • The electrical equipment on the construction site must have explosion-proof equipment. Explosion-proof sockets must be used. Never use knife switch.

    • Provide sufficient fire extinguishers, yellow sand and other fire-fighting equipment on site.

    • Wash hands with soap or sanitizer after the work.
