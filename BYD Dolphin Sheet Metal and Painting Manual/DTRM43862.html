Terms

Glossary

Terms

Interpretation

Measuring system

Multiple measuring instruments which are used in combination to comprehensively measure the whole vehicle body. For example, a measuring system composed of measuring appliance or laser beam and three-dimensional measuring instrument is usually used in conjunction with the body alignment system.

Pneumatic spot welding tongs

Tongs using compressed air pressure to open or close the spot welder arm, independent of manual pressure, which can apply sufficient welding pressure.

Pneumatic tools

Tools powered by compressed air, which have many types and a wide range of applications in grinding, cutting, installation and removal.

Chisel

Used to separate panels or remove welded panels. It has a cutting blade at one end and is used for hammering at the other end.

Fixture

A tool for fixing objects, which is often used to flatten the panel or material, and secure the welding fixture for panels during welding.

Hammer

An indispensable tool for repair of the body. Knocking the panel with a hammer is called "hammering".

Slide hammer

A drawing tool formed by the reaction force of a sliding weight connected to the handle. The slide hammer is used for drawing washers, or directly welded to the panel to draw.

Grinder

A general term for grinding tools, which usually refers to pneumatic grinders. There are many types of grinders, such as disk grinder, ring grinder and double-action grinder, which shall be selected according to specific purposes.

Belt grinder

The belt grinder utilizes annular band-type sandpaper to remove the paint layer on spot welding parts, which can be used for grinding in narrow spaces.

Hand tools

Wrenches, screwdrivers and other handheld tools that do not require compressed air or electrical energy.

Bucking block

Used for shaping the panel and placed against the back of the part to be knocked. It is a metal block, with different shapes.

Air

It refers to the atmosphere originally, but usually refers to compressed air for repair of the body.

Carbon dioxide (CO2)

A colorless and odorless gas composed of carbon and oxygen, with a specific gravity higher than that of air. It is used as shielding gas during CO2 arc welding.

Inert gas

Used to isolate the welding position. During arc welding, it is ejected from the welding gun. This gas is free of chemical combination with other substances. It can be replaced by CO2 gas for welding steel panels. However, argon must be used for welding aluminum alloy and stainless steel.

CO2 gas shielded welding machine

By using carbon dioxide or mixed gas of carbon dioxide and argon as shielding gas to isolate the welding area, so as to make it more reliable.

Spot welder

Often used to replace welded outer panels by concentrating large currents on and pressing against an overlap point of a panel. It is easy to operate and does not likely cause the panel to be deformed after welding.

Oxyacetylene welder

A welding machine that uses the heat generated by combustion of oxyacetylene mixture for welding. It cannot be used for welding panels, which makes panels heated greatly and easily deformed.

Welding torch

A tool used for oxyacetylene welder, which can be used for mixing and adjustment of gases. There are many types of flame guns, with different structures for different purposes, such as welding flame gun and cutting flame gun.

Sheet metal shaping and welding machine (meson machine)

Used to weld washers for leveling panels and specially shaped drawing objects and also shrink the steel panel extension.

Pressure gauge

Used to adjust the air pressure to obtain high-pressure gas (oxygen, acetylene and carbon dioxide) from the cylinder.

Line chisel

The main body of this chisel is crescent-shaped and has a handle for correcting the punched lines. During use, it is positioned on the back of the material and knocked with a hammer.

Spotting drill (spot welding drill)

The cutting blade of the drill bit can remove welding spots formed by spot welding. The cutting blade of an ordinary drill bit has a sharp tip, while that of this drill bit has a flat head.

Locking pliers

Used to fix the panels during welding. It can be adjusted so as to fix the panels with a large force.

Welding mask

A mask worn by welders to protect them from strong light and sparks during CO2 gas arc welding.

Steel

An alloy, also known as "carbon steel", whose strength and elasticity are greatly improved by mixing iron, carbon and other elements. The steel can be mass-produced especially, and its characteristics are easily controlled through element composition adjustment. Its weight accounts for more than half of the weight of automobile. The steel panel is produced by rolling steel to be thinner.

High-strength steel panel

A steel panel with higher tensile strength than ordinary steel panels. With given strength, this steel panel can be thinner than ordinary steel panels, thus reducing the weight.

Cold-rolled steel panel

A steel panel obtained by pressing and rolling hot-rolled steel panels at room temperature. It is a kind of thin steel panel with high appearance quality and has been applied to most vehicle bodies.

Hot-rolled steel panel

A steel panel made by heating and rolling steel panels at high temperature

Anti-rust steel panel

A steel panel with thin zinc coating on one or two sides to prevent it from rusting

GOA body

A system used to prevent the driver and passengers from being injured in collision. This system has one or more parts easy to fold and crumple arranged on the vehicle, so as to buffer the collision energy through the deformation of the body in case of collision. This structure is currently adopted in most vehicles.

Unitary body (integral body)

A body structure in which various steel panels are stressed through welding, forming the whole surface of the vehicle, without independent beams

Sealant

A gap filler applied to the joints of welded panels during panel installation. The main sealant types are single-liquid polyurethane sealant and silicone sealant. The sealant is stored in the rubber cylinder and applied with a sealant gun.

Zinc powder conductive primer (spot welding paint)

A conductive rust inhibitor for spot welding, which is diluted by water and applied to the joint of panels

Chassis paint (anti-collision granular paint)

Black anti-rust granular paint applied on the inner side of hubcap and floor base surface. It is very effective in preventing damage to paint layer by gravel, because it has elasticity after drying.

Cavity anti-corrosion wax

A liquid anti-rust product, which is used after the outer panel is repaired or inside the inner cavity of the welded panel. It is used with a spray gun and does not need to be cleaned and dusted before spraying.

Aluminum alloy

Since the aluminum alloy material itself is very brittle and low in strength, it must be mixed with iron or manganese into an alloy for application. It is used in engines, wheels, suspensions and body panels. Compared with engines, steel rings or bodies made of steel panels, those made of aluminum alloy materials have lower weight.

Laminated glass

Two layers of thin glass sheets with plastic interlayer to prevent glass scattering in case of breakage. It is difficult for the glass sheets to pierce through the interlayer.

Tempered glass

It is ten times stronger than ordinary glass and less harmful because it will split into small round fragments when broken. This kind of glass is often used as side window glass and rear window glass.

Polypropylene

PP for short, which is used for manufacturing bumpers and various other accessories inside and outside vehicles

Thermoplastic resin

A resin that can produce expected deformation by heating and softening. This resin is used to make a variety of plastics, such as polypropylene, ABS, polycarbonate, etc.

Thermosetting resin

Plastic polyurethane, polyester and epoxy resin that will harden after heating

Polyurethane

A resin made through reactions of two liquids (called polyurethane reaction). There are several types of rigid resin used to make bumpers, strips, etc., while foam resin is used to make seat cushions, etc.

Body repair

The work of restoring a deformed body to its original shape by drawing, etc. In a broader sense, it should include shaping, panel replacement and configuration adjustment.

Body dimensions

Dimension data or publications of mass-produced bodies issued by the vehicle manufacturer

Panel repair

A shaping mode to restore the deformed panel by knocking with hammer and bucking block. Drawing, as another method, is carried out by means of washer welding, slide hammer, etc.

Warping

Used to describe the state of a panel with extension

Rigidity

A property that materials are not easily deformed under bending or twisting force. Even components of the same material will have different rigidity depending on processing methods.

Elastic deformation

Deformation of the material that can restore to its original shape after the force applied is released

Plastic deformation

A property that materials cannot restore to the original state after the force applied is released. A force greater than the elastic limit is required to restore the material to its original state.

Unaligned hammering

A method of repairing panels by hammer and bucking block, in which the position to be hammered deviates from the bucking block position during knocking

Aligned hammering

A method of repairing panels, in which the hammer shall be aligned with the center of the bucking block during knocking

Weld pass

Welding trace when CO2 arc welding is adopted

Brazing

A welding method of infiltration of low-melting-point metals into the gap between base metals

Pressure welding

A welding method in which melted metal parts are bonded to each other under pressure, such as spot welding

Welding spot

A round pit formed during spot welding. More precisely, it is a welding spot formed by spot welding or fusion welding, which is a round pit on the outer surface of the material welded. The size of the welding spot is proportional to the welding strength.

Fusion welding

A welding method of melting and bonding metal plates together. According to the type of metals to be welded, the welding rod made of suitable materials is required. Both CO2 gas arc welding and gas welding are fusion welding.

Butt welding

A welding method of not overlapping the panels but welding their end faces together. The end faces of panels shall not be tightly close to each other, but with a certain gap.

Plug welding

Suitable for panel structures where spot welding is not available. Through MIG welding, the plug welding holes on the upper panel are filled up.

Lap welding

A welding method of overlapping two or more panels together

Gas welding

A welding method of using an oxyacetylene welder

Splashing

Mainly caused by the oxide of molten metal generated during CO2 gas arc welding. Nozzles blocked by splashings will lead to poor welding performance, so the nozzles shall be cleaned frequently.

Arc

When the relevant conditions are met, electric energy will be transmitted between electrodes through air. In this case, sparks will be generated and accompanied by sound. These sparks are arcs used as welding heat sources.

Shrinking

A process of improving the strength of steel panels, which is to heat the steel panels and concentrate their extension on the heated part, and then realize rapid cooling through air. The most common heating method is to use the electric heat generated by sheet metal shaping welder, or use oxyacetylene as the heating source.

Stress concentration

In case of rapid change in the section of a material (such as sectional area change or section drilling or bending), the stress generated relative to external force will not be uniformly distributed, but concentrated in a specific part. This is called "stress concentration". The part with stress concentration is prone to deformation.

Stress

Resistance generated inside the material to resist external force when subjected to external force

Positioning

Temporarily fixing the panels with welding fixtures or small screws before welding, or by locally welding or fastening, or firmly fixing the panels with bolts for replacement

Rough cutting

The work of cutting the panels away from welding positions during replacement of the panels so as to quickly remove damaged panels

Wheel alignment

A state of adjusting tires (wheels) to be not perpendicular to the road surface and vehicle body, but at a certain angle, which plays a significant role in improving the straight driving of the vehicle and the maneuverability of the steering wheel, including wheel inclination angle, kingpin caster and toe-in

Work hardening

A phenomenon that occurs as long as plastic deformation takes place. The panel has a higher hardness after deformation than before.

Remove and install

Adjustment of gap and flatness between the installed panel and adjacent panels, such as the door and fender

Glued glass

Window glass fixed with adhesive
