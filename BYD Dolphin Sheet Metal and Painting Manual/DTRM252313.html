Coating structures of new vehicle

  1. Key points

    • In the automotive classification and technical publications, the terms "coating" and "baking" are often used to describe the coating structures of a new vehicle.

    • "Coating" indicates that the paint is sprayed; and "baking" indicates that the paint is dried. Therefore, "3C3B" means 3 times of coating and 3 times of baking.

    • Generally speaking, plain paint involves 3C3B, aluminum paint involves 4C3B, and pearl paint involves 4C3B and 5C3B (depending on whether the pearl mica pigment and tinting pigment are coated together or separately).

    • Since the original paint does not contain poly-putty, the "coating" and "baking" here do not involve poly-putty, which is different from repair paint.

Coating process of original paint

  1. Key points

Pre-treatment

  1. Key points

    • In the process, a zinc phosphate film is formed on the metal plate to enhance the rust resistance of the body metal plate and ensure the adhesion of primer.

    • This process includes cleaning, degreasing and phosphating.

    • There are two phosphating types: spraying type in which the chemical agent is applied with a nozzle, and full immersion type in which the vehicle body is completely immersed into the chemical agent tank.

  2. Characteristics of full immersion type

    • As the whole body is immersed, a paint film can be formed in hidden areas inside the door and side panels, which are prone to rust.

    • This method ensures that the vehicle body is uniformly exposed to the chemical reagent. The zinc phosphate film produced in this way is composed of fine and high-density crystal structure, which improves rust resistance.

Primer process

  1. Key points

    • In modern automotive plants, primer coating or electrophoresis (ED) coating is to immerse the vehicle body in a charged electrophoretic paint solvent and then apply an electric current to the vehicle body. The charged paint particles are deposited on the active surface of the vehicle body. A uniform paint film is formed on the whole body surface, including intersections and recesses of thin plates. The rust resistance of the vehicle body is improved due to the deposition of paint when the vehicle body is immersed in solvent.

    • The paint is charged in the solvent, and the object to be coated is immersed in the tank. A direct current is applied between the object and the paint tank to move the paint particles towards the object (this process is called electromigration).

    • After the paint is absorbed by the object surface, the charge-neutralized paint is deposited on the plate surface through condensation (this process is called electrodeposition).

    • The moisture in the paint is reduced due to electrolytic separation from the coating (this process is called electroosmosis).

    Reminder

    • There are two types of electrocoating, i.e. anodic electrophoresis and cathodic electrophoresis.

    • In anodic electrophoresis, the vehicle body is the anode, and the battery is the cathode. In the aqueous solution, resin is negatively charged, showing a negative ion state. When an electric current is applied, the resin accumulates on the positively charged vehicle body surface and loses the negative charge, allowing the insoluble resin components to deposit. The zinc phosphate produced with the previous process is attracted by the negative charge of the paint and dissolved in the paint, reducing the film, therefore, the anodic electrophoresis makes the rust resistance of vehicle body decreased.

    • In cathodic electrophoresis, the vehicle body is the cathode, and the battery is the anode. The resin paint that can become positive ions in water is used.

    • In cathodic electrophoresis, the rust resistance obtained with this method is better than that obtained with the anodic electrophoresis method since the zinc phosphate film produced in the previous process does not dissolve. Cathodic electrophoresis is used in many automobile electrophoretic coating processes.

Primer coating and sealing process

  1. Key points

    • After electrophoretic coating, the underbody shall be painted to improve the sand and gravel impact resistance and rust resistance, prevent water from entering the vehicle, and isolate noise and vibration.

    • There are two types of primers: solvent-based - asphalt + rubberized resin + inorganic filler + solvent, and sol-based - PVC + inorganic filler + plasticizer.

    Reminder

    • Now the sol type is the most widely used. After painting, the vehicle body is placed in the intermediate primer oven, and the polyvinyl chloride sol is thermoset to form a thick elastic film.

    • The body sealant consists of the following materials: PVC solvent, inorganic filler and plastisol. The sealant is compressed with an airless pump, and a manual sealant gun is used to apply the sealant in a stack manner. Robots are now also used for automatic painting.

Coating process of intermediate paint

  1. Key points

    • The intermediate paint features three main functions: increasing the adhesion between electrophoretic paint and top coat, making the surface smoother, and sealing the surface for preparation of top coat spraying.

    • The coating process of intermediate paint is performed after coating of body seals and fixing asphalt plates. It aims to enhance the adhesion between electrophoretic layer and top coat layer, improve stress absorption and ensure smooth surface of top coat. This process is also used for interior coating of engine compartment and trunk, as well as black matte (semi-gloss) coating of door frames.

    • In the modern manufacturing process, an electrostatic coating system is adopted, and the intermediate paint is sprayed by robots and automatic painting equipment.

    • The intermediate paint can be divided into nitro intermediate paint, urethane intermediate paint and thermosetting amino alkyd intermediate paint.

    • The nitro intermediate paint and thermosetting amino alkyd intermediate paint are one-component paint, while the urethane intermediate paint is two-component paint. The nitro intermediate paint and urethane intermediate paint are generally used as repair paint, while the thermosetting amino alkyd intermediate paint is generally used as original paint.

Coating process of intermediate paint

  1. Key points

    • Polish the defective areas in primer coating to improve the aesthetics after coating of top coat.

    • In the original vehicle process, the intermediate paint has good spraying flatness. Generally, spot grinding is adopted to treat dust spots and defects on the surface before coating of top coat.

    • Grind to remove eluents, oxide layers, impurities and surface roughness formed during baking. Form a flat and solid surface on the intermediate paint surface, and grind the surface with #400 sandpaper to ensure the adhesion and smoothness required for subsequent painting process.

    Reminder

    • In the paint repairing process, the dry grinding process is generally used, because dry grinding can guarantee the quality of the repair process.

Top coat process

  1. Key points

    • The color of a vehicle is determined in this process, so its result greatly affects the value of the vehicle. In addition, the top coat must be able to withstand changes in external conditions such as aging while maintaining its appearance.

    • The electrostatic coating method is adopted for the original top coat, and the whole process includes covering, cleaning, top coat spraying, curing, drying, visual inspection, black coating and drying.

    Reminder

    • The painting work of most new cars is done by automatic painting equipment.

    • Some processes are jointly performed by automatic painting equipment and robots.

    • Computers are used to control changes in models and colors, so that the same assembly line can adapt to different models or colors of vehicles.

Relevant characteristics of metallic paint

Characteristics of metallic paint

  1. Key points

    • Unlike plain paints that only use tinting pigments and present the same color at different observation angles, metallic paints contain glittering pigments with special gloss under light and can exhibit different colors at different observation angles.

Film structure of metallic paint

  1. Key points

    • It produces a unique glittering effect in sunlight.

    • When viewed from the front or side, there is a great difference in the hue.

    • It exhibits excellent transparency and three-dimensional effect.

    • Compared with plain paint, metallic paint produces different effects.

    • Difference between metallic paint and plain paint: different number of processes and pigments used, and obviously different reflective effect.

Difference in wet and dry coating of metallic paint

  1. Key points

    • Color effects of different coating methods (wet coating on the left side and dry coating on the right side).

    • Aluminum powder pigment shows glittering phenomenon due to its reflective characteristics.

    • When applying paint on the color sample plate, be sure to keep the application conditions unchanged.

    • When applying paint on the color sample plate, its application conditions shall be the same as those of the actual vehicle.

    • Color change caused by difference between wet and dry coatings: In case of wet spraying, the surface is dark when viewed directly and bright when viewed obliquely; in case of dry spraying, the opposite is true.

Metallic paint flashes

  1. Key points

    • Metallic paint is different from plain paint. The tinting pigment and aluminum pigment used have flash particles with special gloss under light exposure, and present different colors from different angles.

Key points of metallic paint application

  1. Key points

    • Aluminum and mica pigments used in metallic paints are more prone to precipitation because they are heavier than ordinary tinting pigments, and it is important that the paint be thoroughly stirred before being poured into a spray gun.

    • As the particles of aluminum powder pigment are larger than those of tinting pigments, they shall be filtered with filter screens of suitable mesh to ensure that aluminum powder particles will not be filtered out.

    • Generally speaking, #80 to #100 funnels are used for intermediate paint and metallic primer, #150 to #180 funnels are used for plain paint, and #180 to #200 funnels are used for varnish.

Principle of metallic paint spots

  1. Key points

    • The uneven distribution of glitter pigments and the uneven thickness and glossiness of coatings lead to uneven reflection light in various colors (blurring or spotting).

    • Common spots include two types: uneven spraying of aluminum powder and reflux of aluminum paint.

Types of metallic spots

  1. Key points

    • Uneven aluminum powder: If the spraying conditions are inconsistent, the arrangement state of aluminum powder in the aluminum paint coating is inconsistent, and the reflection to light is inconsistent, thus the spots will be formed and seen.

    • Aluminum power reflux: The spots will also occur if the varnish is sprayed immediately after the primer is sprayed. Because the aluminum powder in the bottom layer is dissolved by the solvent in the varnish layer, it will mix with the coating varnish layer to cause spots.

    • Spots of primer coating: It is produced when the aluminum powder in the coating diffuses unevenly. In order to eliminate the spots, spray the paint almost dry. However, if the coating is too dry, a dry coating will be produced and the color will change.

    • Methods to remove spots: Generally, it is solved by a medium-dry spraying method. In order to prevent uneven aluminum powder, the four parameters of spray gun operation should be maintained, and the coating thickness should be uniform.

    • Timing for removing spots: After the primer is sprayed, the solvent in the coating film begins to volatilize. The brightness of the wet coating film decreases and the matte finish reaches 50%~70% of the original glossiness, then apply metallic paint to remove spots.

    • Varnish layer spots: The metallic paint is applied with the varnish over the primer. If the varnish is applied immediately after the aluminum paint is applied, it will cause spots. Therefore, a slightly longer interlayer standing time shall be allowed after spraying aluminum paint. After confirming that the primer is dry, properly spray the varnish coating and avoid over-wetting.

Darkening of the blend area

  1. Key points

    • When the edge of the blend area of aluminum paint dries, it can cause the surface color to darken when viewed obliquely.

  2. Countermeasures.

    • During transition, apply a blend resin coating on the surface of the blend area.

    • Blend resin can avoid the problem of thinning coating edges and quick drying of coating during blend area spraying, and make the coating surface smooth while enhancing the adhesion of other primers and preventing color darkening.

    Reminder

    • For information on how to use the splice transition resin, refer to the production description of the paint manufacturer.

    • When applying plastic parts, control the electrostatic phenomenon on the coating surface to reduce the occurrence of spots and color darkening.

Application process of solvent-based metallic paint

Characteristics of metallic primer

  1. Key points

    • Aluminum powder pigments and mica pigments are heavier than tinting pigments. They have the following characteristics: easy precipitation of aluminum paint particles, a large difference in front and side lights after spraying, and prone to spot problems.

    • Compared with plain paint, aluminum paint requires better painting skills and application precautions.

    Reminder

    • Solvent-based metallic paint and solid paint differs in the composition of the paint film and the pigments used, as well as the coating method of metallic paint. Since the metallic paint consists of two layers (aluminum powder paint layer + varnish layer), the aluminum powder paint has the function of covering the substrate and providing color effect in the coating, and the varnish layer forms textures and gloss.

An introduction to the whole plate spraying process

  1. Operating procedures

    • The whole plate spraying process is shown in the figure.

Mist spraying

  1. Key points

    • Apply a thin layer of primer on the intermediate paint application area.

    • Check the substrate for defects. If any, increase the air pressure and dry spray to cover it.

    Reminder

    • Proceed to colored spraying after the preset flash-off time ends.

Coloring spraying

  1. Key points

    • Wet spray the primer coating to make the paint film glossy.

    • When the covering power of the base coat is poor, we can spray it several times until the primer is completely covered.

    • Check and ensure that the substrate is completely covered after spraying.

    Reminder

    • Spray evenly to form a uniform film (if the coating thickness is uneven, it will affect the "dust-free" degree and make subsequent stain removal spraying more difficult).

Remove the spots

  1. Key points

    • Through this step, the blurring (spotting) phenomenon is eliminated.

  2. Steps

    • When the paint is free from dust adhesion, the mist-sprayed base coat layer enables the paint to be almost dry-sprayed onto the surface of the panel, ensuring a consistent arrangement of aluminum powder particles.

    • After spraying, check whether there are spots. If the spots are not removed, repeat the first step.

    Reminder

    • If the spot removal time is too early, spot removal will fail. On the contrary, if the spot is corrected too late, the aluminum powder paint will be inconsistently arranged, resulting in an unsightly metallic powder.

    • Observe the gloss to form a certain amount of uniform paint film, achieving approximately 50% of the gloss.

Mist spraying of varnish

  1. Key points

    • Mist spray the varnish to form a film (to prevent spots).

    • Before mist spraying the varnish, make sure that the primer has dried thoroughly.

Varnish texture decoration spraying

  1. Key points

    • Apply a uniform coat of varnish to ensure excellent fullness and gloss, and match the texture of adjacent panels.

    Reminder

    • Allow sufficient flash-off time before spraying.

    • Never apply an excessively thick coating. Otherwise it will cause sagging and spots.

Drying

  1. Key points

    • Allow a standing time of 10-20 minutes, then carry out forced drying at about 60 °C for about 30 minutes.

    Reminder

    • Set the drying conditions according to the instructions in the paint product manual.

  1. Operating procedures

Mist spraying

  1. Key points

    • Apply a thin layer of mist spray on the intermediate paint area.

    Reminder

    • After spraying, check the substrate for defects, and increase the air pressure for dry spraying to cover them (if any).

Coloring spraying

  1. Key points

    • Spray several times until the primer coat is completely covered.

    • The spraying area needs to be appropriately increased for each recoating.

    Reminder

    • Clear the paint on neighboring areas with the tack cloth and reduce paint dust.

    • Spray the base coat evenly to ensure an even coating.

Blend area spraying

  1. Key points

    • Eliminate spots by medium-dry sprayed mist coating.

    • In this operation, the removal of spots is carried out together with the junction operation.

    Reminder

    • Observe the gloss to form uniform film effect. If there is any color spot, correct it during transition spraying.

    • Expand the spraying area during each spraying, gradually reduce the coating thickness and transition the color.

    • Wipe with the tack cloth between spraying of coatings to avoid paint dust and aluminum powder particles in the splice area.

Mist spraying of varnish

  1. Key points

    • Mist spray varnish over the area that is larger than the base coat blend area.

    • Check the aluminum paint coating for defects before spraying.

    Reminder

    • Pay attention to the timing of varnish application. If the varnish is applied too early, the aluminum powder particles may float into the varnish and form spots; if the varnish is applied too late, the paint will be over-dried, causing poor adhesion.

Texture decoration spraying

  1. Key points

    • Spray varnish coating evenly and adjust the texture effect of the paint film after spraying.

    Reminder

    • Reserve sufficient flash-off time before application.

    • Avoid sagging and orange peel.

Drying

  1. Key points

    • Allow a standing time of 10-20 minutes, then carry out forced drying at about 60 °C for about 30 minutes.

    Reminder

    • Perform forced drying for the varnish coating after standstill drying.

Water-based metallic paint spraying on the whole plate

Mist spraying of primer

  1. Operation steps

    • Spray a single coating to achieve approximately 50% glossiness on the surface after spraying.

    • Check for defects such as fish eyes after spraying.

    • Use a special blower to dry the paint until the surface is no longer glossy.

  2. Standard

    • Make sure that the glossiness of the sprayed area is about 50% after spraying.

    • Make sure that the coating is no longer glossy after drying.

  3. Key points

    • After spraying, wait for a few seconds before assessing the glossiness.

    • Illuminate the surface with a fluorescent lamp and check whether the surface is glossy from an oblique angle to confirm the dryness of the surface.

    Caution

    • The substrate does not absorb paint. The excessive gloss will cause sagging.

    • The uneven gloss will result in coarse textures in the coating.

    • A long duration of drying is required in case of high humidity and low temperature. Carefully check the gloss to avoid sagging.

Coloring spraying

  1. Steps

    • Spray the surface to achieve a glossiness of about 60-70% immediately after spraying.

    • Whenever you complete the spray of one coating, you should dry the coating with a blower until the surface is no longer glossy.

    • Repeat spraying as many times as necessary until the substrate of the plate is covered.

  2. Key points

    • The spray gun should be closer to the surface than the mist spraying.

    • Check carefully whether there is incomplete drying in the areas where coatings are easy to deposit, such as panel edges.

    • As the number of coatings increases, the coating becomes thicker and the drying time also gets longer.

    Caution

    • The insufficient gloss after application will result in coarse textures.

    • Pinholes will occur if application is carried out while the previous layer has not been dried completely.

    • Pinholes will occur or the gloss of paint film will be decreased if application is carried out while the previous layer has not been dried completely.

Remove the spots

  1. Steps

    • Make sure that the applied surface is flat with a glossiness of about 30%-50%.

    • Use a blower gun to dry each coating until the surface is not glossy.

    • Check and confirm that the coating surface is free of spots and blurring.

  2. Standard

    • When applying, ensure that the coating is flat with a glossiness of about 30%-50%.

    • After drying, check and confirm that the coating is free of spots or blurring.

  3. Key points

    • Make the distance between the spray gun and the surface greater than that required for coloring spraying.

    • If the spots on the surface still remain after more than two times of spraying, it is necessary to conduct the spot-removing spraying again after coloring spraying.

    Caution

    • Dry spraying makes it difficult for the paint mist to adhere, resulting in coarse surface textures and spots.

Mist spraying of varnish coating

  1. Steps

    • Keep the spraying conditions stable during spraying.

    • Reserve sufficient time for flash drying to ensure complete solvent evaporation.

  2. Standard

    • Make sure that the paint is applied evenly and that the surface has a uniform gloss.

    • Make sure the surface is dry to the extent that allows touching.

  3. Key points

    • Check the drying condition according to the paint film on the shelters around the sprayed panels or the area covered by the assembly parts, so as not to affect the coating quality.

    • Spray the edges of the panel before spraying the whole panel.

    Caution

    • Uneven mist spraying may result in sagging or coarse texture.

Varnish texture decoration spraying

  1. Steps

    • When spraying, ensure that the texture is consistent with that of the original part of the accessory after spraying.

    • Allow sufficient flash drying time for the solvent to evaporate, maintain stable spraying conditions and check the texture at any time.

  2. Key points

    • Divide the panel into three parts and spray one part at a time.

    • After spraying each area, repeat spraying in areas with inconsistent texture.

    • Illuminate surfaces with fluorescent lamps or other lighting to check the texture effect.

    Caution

    • If spraying of the zone with inconsistent textures is carried out after spraying of the whole plate surface, the paint mist will fail to penetrate into the coating, thus resulting in coat textures finally.

    Reminder

    • Observe instructions on spraying conditions in the product manual.

Drying

  1. Key points

    • Before forced drying of the sprayed coating, allow a certain curing time.

    Reminder

    • Observe instructions on drying conditions in the product manual.

    • Curing time: the duration reserved before drying for volatilization of solvents in the paint.
