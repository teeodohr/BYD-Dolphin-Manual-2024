Composition of Coating

Paint composition

  1. Key points

    • Paint is a liquid composed of resins, pigments, additives, solvents, etc., usually diluted with diluent to the viscosity suitable for painting before being used.

    • Paints are divided into colored paint added with pigment and colorless transparent varnish without pigment.

Resin

  1. Key points

    • Resin is a transparent liquid used to increase gloss, hardness, and adhesion. This component can make the liquid paint form a "paint film" after the drying and curing process.

    • Resin is generally divided into natural resin and synthetic resin. It is one of the main components constituting the paint film. Its properties determine the final effect of the paint and the performance of the paint film. It is the most important component in the paint film.

    • Natural resin is generally extracted from animals and plants, such as shellac and turpentine, etc.

    • Synthetic resin is mainly extracted from petroleum.

Pigment

  1. Key points

    • The pigment is powdery and can provide color for the coating and increase its thickness. The pigment is insoluble in solvents and is added to the paint to provide color and fillibility.

    • Pigment is added to the paint to make the paint film with color, coverage, and fillibility.

    • Pigment is an extremely fine powdery colored substance insoluble in water, oil, and solvent. It is usually divided into organic pigment and inorganic pigment.

  2. Classification by purpose.

    • Tinting pigment: coloring.

    • Bright pigment increases gloss, such as metallic color or pearlescent effect.

    • Body pigment increases the coating thickness.

    • Anti-rust pigment has anti-rust effect.

    • Matting pigment reduces resin gloss.

Additives

  1. Key points

    • It is used to prevent bubbles, pigment precipitation or improve coating performance. There are mainly two types of additives. One is used by paint manufacturers, and the other is used by sprayers.

    • Paint manufacturers use additives to optimize the performance of paint products.

    • Pigment dispersant helps to disperse pigments and prevent the dispersed pigments from polymerizing with adhesive.

    • Ultraviolet absorbent absorbs ultraviolet rays to avoid deterioration of the paint surface caused by sunlight.

    • Defoaming agents prevent the formation of foam, which will form bubbles during the paint spraying operation.

    • Leveling agents enable the paint to level out and facilitate the formation of a smooth coating.

    • Anti-settling agent evenly disperses the pigment in the paint to avoid uneven color concentration caused by paint precipitation.

    • Sprayers use additives to change the characteristics of paint.

    • Soft additive changes the elasticity of intermediate coat, top coat, and varnish.

    • Drying accelerators accelerate the chemical drying of the two-component intermediate coat, top coat, and varnish at low temperatures or in minor repair scenarios.

    • Matting agents reduce the gloss of the top coat or varnish.

    • The anti-fish eye solution prevents the occurrence of fisheyes when the paint or the surface contains contaminants.

    Reminder
    Additives mainly include: plasticizer, curing agent, antisettling agent, anti flocculant, flatting agent, defoamer, etc.

Solvents

  1. Key points

    • A solvent is a liquid that dissolves resin and helps blend pigments. The solvent is volatile, so there will be no solvent residue after the coating is dried.

    • As the viscosity of the resin is very high, a solvent is used to dilute it to the appropriate viscosity, giving it fluidity and spreadability. Depending on the type of resin, the solvents used vary. In addition, the solvent can completely evaporate after the paint is dried and there will be no solvent residue in the paint film.

    • Different resins use different solvents as dissolving agents and diluents. When in use, the diluent suitable for the evaporation speed should be selected according to the operation temperature. For example, diluents are classified into different types according to the evaporation speed, including fast, medium, slow, and extremely slow speed diluents.
